# 🍎 Graph That Motion — Teacher Guide & Lesson Plan
**The Thinking Experiment | PhysicsKit Kinematics Module**

---

## 📋 Overview & Pedagogical Purpose
This module targets the most persistent misconceptions in high school kinematics:
1. **Graph-as-Picture Fallacy:** Confusing a graph curve with a physical hill or road.
2. **Position vs. Velocity Confusion:** Believing a "high" point on a graph means the object is moving fast.
3. **Slope vs. Height Confusion:** Failing to recognize that steepness represents instantaneous velocity on $x$-$t$ and acceleration on $v$-$t$.
4. **Sign of Acceleration Confusion:** Assuming negative acceleration always means "slowing down" rather than considering the sign of velocity.

## 🔑 Complete Answer Key (Graphs A through K)

| Graph | Type | Exact Motion Breakdown | Common Student Pitfalls |
| :---: | :---: | :--- | :--- |
| **A** | $x$-$t$ | Starts from $x=0$, $v_0=0$, $a > 0$. Curves upward with increasing steepness. | Students mistake straight line for speeding up. |
| **B** | $v$-$t$ | $v = -4\text{ m/s}$ ($1.4\text{s}$) $\rightarrow$ $v = 0$ ($1.2\text{s}$) $\rightarrow$ speeds up to $+5\text{ m/s}$. | Students confuse $v=0$ on axis with "at the origin". |
| **C** | $v$-$t$ | Constant line at $v = -4.2\text{ m/s}$ below the 0-axis. | Students confuse negative velocity with slowing down. |
| **D** | $x$-$t$ | Starts far right ($x=18.5$), slows down left to stop $\rightarrow$ rests $\rightarrow$ constant speed left. | Confusing flattening curve with moving right. |
| **E** | $x$-$t$ | Straight upward line from $x=0$, constant $v = +4.2\text{ m/s}$. | Clean baseline constant positive velocity. |
| **F** | $v$-$t$ | $v$ slopes down from $0$ to $-5\text{ m/s}$ $\rightarrow$ $v=0$ $\rightarrow$ constant $v = -4.5\text{ m/s}$. | Students miss that downward slope in $v$-$t$ is negative acceleration. |
| **G** | $x$-$t$ | Const speed right $\rightarrow$ rest $\rightarrow$ const speed left (peak). | Students think the car went up a hill. |
| **H** | $v$-$t$ | Starts at $v = +8.5\text{ m/s}$, linear downward slope to $v=0$. | Students confuse downward slope on $v$-$t$ with moving backward. |
| **I** | $x$-$t$ | Starts from rest at $x=18.5$, concave down curve moving to $x=2$. | Confusing concave down with slowing down. |
| **J** | $x$-$t$ | Const speed right $\rightarrow$ rest $\rightarrow$ speeds up right (curves up). | Distinguishing linear segment from parabolic curve. |
| **K** | $x$-$t$ | Starts fast moving left ($x=18.5$), curves up to flat horizontal at stop. | Sign of acceleration: $v < 0$ and $a > 0 \implies$ slowing down. |

## 🏫 4 Active Classroom Facilitation Strategies

### 1. Four Corners Kinesthetic Match (15–20 min)
- Project the simulation at the front of the classroom.
- Post cards **A, B, C, D** on the 4 walls.
- Play a motion (e.g. Motion #4). Students have 30s to discuss, then walk to their chosen corner.
- Call on one spokesperson from each corner to defend their choice using physics evidence ("*The car's motion dots were getting closer together, so the slope must flatten out...*").

### 2. Whiteboard Prediction Sketching (10–15 min)
- Have students work in pairs with mini-whiteboards.
- Play the motion. Students must draw their predicted $x$-$t$ and $v$-$t$ graphs *before* any letter choices are revealed.
- Reveal the 11 graphs and have them identify their match.

### 3. Socratic Diagnostic Debriefs
When students make a mistake, use the simulation's built-in **Diagnostic Engine**:
- *Teacher Prompt:* "Notice you picked Graph H for Motion #3. Look at the y-axis. Does Graph H tell us *where* the car is, or *how fast* it is moving?"
- *Scrubbing Feature:* Have the student drag the **Time Scrubber** to $t = 1.5\text{ s}$ and observe the live tracer dot and velocity vector simultaneously.

### 4. Custom Student Scenario Peer-Challenge
- Direct students to the **🛠️ Scenario Builder** tab.
- Each student constructs a unique 2-stage or 3-stage motion and challenges their desk partner to sketch the corresponding graphs.
